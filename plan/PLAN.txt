# OxMarket — prediction market for Oxford University news (OxHack, theme: TIME)

## Context
Hackathon build. You want a play-money prediction market whose markets are generated from Oxford student news (Cherwell, The Isis, OxStu/Oxford Student, Cuppers sport coverage), running on your existing C++ limit order book (`book/OrderBook`). Stack: simple backend (Flask), plain HTML/CSS/JS frontend. Main prize targets: **MLH Best Use of Tiger Data** (TimescaleDB as the time-series core, which also fits the TIME theme) and **Best Use of Gemini API** (scraping articles into markets, writing summaries of how markets have moved). Secondary: HRT "Data for Predictions", Dev Writing Challenge (via the dev log).

Decisions you've confirmed: new schema `oxmarket` in Timescale; a house market-maker bot for liquidity; one YES book per market where "Buy NO @ x" = "Sell YES @ (100−x)".

## 1. Architecture

```
Browser (HTML/CSS/JS, polls every 2s)
      │ JSON REST
Flask app (server/)
  ├─ engine.py ── stdin/stdout ──► MarketServer.exe (your C++ book, one OrderBook per market)
  ├─ risk.py      (cash/collateral checks before anything hits the engine)
  ├─ db.py ─────────────────────► TimescaleDB, schema `oxmarket`
  ├─ scraper.py   (RSS/HTML → articles)
  ├─ ai.py        (Gemini: generate markets, write market briefs, suggest resolutions)
  └─ jobs.py      (background threads: scrape, tick snapshots, MM bot, auto-close)
```

- **The C++ engine only does matching.** Python is the source of truth for balances (in Postgres). It checks risk *before* sending an order and applies each fill the engine reports.
- **Bridge = subprocess over stdin/stdout**: no pybind11, no extra C++ dependencies, works with CLion's bundled MinGW. A single `threading.Lock` around the engine keeps every trade in strict order.
- **Restarts:** on boot, Flask replays all `open` orders from Postgres into a fresh engine process.

**Protocol** (one command per line, response ends with `END`):

## 3. Prediction-market rules (play money)
- New accounts get **£1,000 = 100,000p** of fake money. Prices are 1–99p and a YES share pays 100p if the outcome is YES, otherwise 0.
- Positions are signed (negative means short YES, i.e. long NO).
- **Risk check** (`risk.py`): `available = cash − 100·Σ short shares − Σ open buy reservations (p·q) − Σ open short-sell collateral ((100−p)·q beyond long holdings)`. Orders that exceed `available` are rejected.
- Market orders: preview `BOOK` depth for the worst-case cost and cap the quantity. Any unfilled remainder is dropped (IOC).
- Resolution: cash += position × (100 if YES else 0). All open orders are cancelled and the market is marked `resolved`. An admin confirms (with a Gemini suggestion, §6).

## 4. TimescaleDB (Tiger Data prize centrepiece), schema `oxmarket`
Safety: step 0 is read-only (list schemas/extensions, confirm `oxmarket` doesn't exist and `timescaledb` is installed). Every DDL statement is schema-qualified `oxmarket.*`. Nothing is ever ALTERed or DROPped outside it. No `CREATE EXTENSION` if it already exists. The password goes only in `.env` (gitignored).

Tables (`server/schema.sql`, idempotent `IF NOT EXISTS`):
- `users(id, username, pw_hash, cash, is_admin, is_bot, created_at)`
- `markets(id, question, resolution_criteria, category, source_name, source_url, article_id, closes_at, status[proposed|open|closed|resolved|rejected], outcome, ai_prior, created_at)`
- `articles(id, source, url UNIQUE, title, summary, published_at, scraped_at)`
- `orders(engine_order_id, market_id, user_id, side, price, qty, qty_remaining, status, created_at)`
- `positions(user_id, market_id, qty)`
- `ai_briefs(market_id, created_at, kind[brief|resolution], text)`
- **Hypertables:** `trades(time, market_id, price, qty, buyer_id, seller_id, taker_side)`, `price_ticks(time, market_id, bid, ask, mid, last)` (snapshot every 15s plus after each trade), `portfolio_snapshots(time, user_id, equity)` (every 5 min).
- **Continuous aggregates:** `candles_5m` and `candles_1h` built with `time_bucket` + `first/last/max/min/sum(qty)` on trades, with refresh policies.
- **Compression policy** on `price_ticks` older than 1 day, plus a retention example.
- **Queries:** `time_bucket_gapfill` + `locf` for smooth charts. "As-of" queries for the Time Machine. `last()` for current prices. Leaderboard history from `portfolio_snapshots`.

## 5. TIME-themed features
- **Time Machine page:** a slider across the market's lifetime shows every market's probability at time T (gapfill/locf), plus the headlines published up to T.
- **Countdown** on every market (`closes_at`). Markets auto-close at the deadline. Markets are framed as "by <date>" questions.
- **Probability-over-time chart** (Chart.js via CDN) with 5m/1h candle toggles from the continuous aggregates.
- **"Hindsight" leaderboard:** each user's equity over time, and who called it earliest.

## 6. Gemini (`ai.py`, `google-genai` SDK, model set by `GEMINI_MODEL` env var; verify the current flash model name when building)
1. **Market generation:** new articles go to Gemini with a JSON schema response of `{question, resolution_criteria, closes_at, category, prior_probability, reason}`, or `null` if the article isn't marketable. Results land as `status=proposed` in an **admin approval queue** (avoids junk or defamatory markets; prompt forbids markets about named private students). The prior seeds the MM bot's fair value.
2. **Market brief, "why did this move?":** input is the last 24h of `time_bucket` series plus related articles. Output is a short summary, cached in `ai_briefs` and refreshed at most every 30 min or on large moves.
3. **Resolution assistant:** for closed markets, newer articles go to a suggested outcome plus evidence link. The admin clicks to confirm.
4. Optional: a daily "Oxford in odds" digest on the homepage.

## 7. Scraper (`scraper.py`)
- RSS first (WordPress `/feed/`): Cherwell (cherwell.org, plus the sport category for Cuppers), The Isis (isismagazine.org.uk), Oxford Student/OxStu (oxfordstudent.com). Exact feed URLs get checked in step 0. Fallback is `requests` + BeautifulSoup on the section pages.
- Polls every 30 min with a polite User-Agent. Stores headline, summary and link only, no full article reproduction. Dedupes on URL.
- `POST /api/admin/scrape` triggers it manually for the demo.

## 8. House market-maker bot (`jobs.py`)
- `house` bot user with large cash. Every 10s, for each open market: cancel its old quotes, then quote bid `fair−2` / ask `fair+2`, size 50, clamped to 1–99.
- `fair` = Gemini prior at first, then an EMA of recent trade prices. Inventory skew shifts quotes against its position.
- Goes through the same risk check → engine path as users.

## 9. Flask API (`app.py`)
`/api/register, /login, /logout, /me` (session cookie, werkzeug password hashing) · `GET /api/markets` · `GET /api/markets/<id>` (book, recent trades, brief) · `GET /api/markets/<id>/history?bucket=5m` · `POST /api/orders` `{market_id, outcome: YES|NO, side: buy|sell, type: limit|market, price, qty}` (NO is mapped to YES) · `DELETE /api/orders/<id>` · `GET /api/portfolio` · `GET /api/leaderboard` · `GET /api/timemachine?t=` · admin: `/api/admin/proposed`, `approve`, `reject`, `resolve`, `scrape`. Static files are served from `web/`.
